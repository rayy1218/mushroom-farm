# Harvest and yield: UI/UX critique and audit (2026-11-04)

Method: Impeccable critique and audit. A (the design review of the live pages) was done in this context. B (the detector scan: files, and the 5 URLs in the detector's own headless browser at 1280 and 390 px) ran as a separate agent. Its report came late and is folded into the Notes. Test bench: this worktree's server on port 18741 with a throwaway database: 2 workers; Room 1 grey oyster 500 blocks with a past run; Room 2 pink oyster; Room 3 grey oyster plus lion's mane; Room 4 empty; about 95 harvests over 100 days; one harvest check Done with kg for each crop; Yield set for grey, black and pink oyster, lion's mane left unset; no energy readings (the gateway was not connected). Screens were checked at 1440 and 390 px (the top bar also at 360 px), in both themes. Screenshots are in `/tmp/hv-audit/shots/`. Print media could not be emulated, so the print rules were checked by reading the CSS.

## Verdict

The feature is a sound build in the house style. It uses the same cards, tiles, tables, segmented control, dialogs and pictures as Usage, Tasks and Calendar. The numbers use tabular figures, and the `--s-harvest` ochre is distinct from the other colours and works in both themes. The top bar fits at 360 px. It is not yet modern and polished, for three reasons:

1. **Settings → Yield looks broken.** Its inputs get no styles, so the browser draws them grey, square and 24 px tall.
2. **Some numbers mislead.** "RM 0.00 per kg" shows when no energy was recorded. Value shows "—" for a whole room or the whole farm when only one crop has no price. "The last 365 days" covers 339 days. "Lion's mane (draft)" appears on records and on the official myGAP sheet.
3. **The phone layouts are a desk layout squeezed down.** The Harvests list costs about 1,700 px of scrolling. The By room table wraps "Room / 1" onto two lines. The Flushes table cuts off its BE column.

Fixing 1 to 10 below brings the feature up to the standard of Calendar and the Room page. 11 to 24 are polish.
